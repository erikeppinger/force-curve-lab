# Reading list

Papers found but not yet read, for the open items. Each was seen in search results only: check the numbers in the paper before using them. Nothing here is cited in the exercise files yet.

## Calf (gastrocnemius): knee straight vs bent

For a `strengthScale` on the standing and seated calf raise. The base curve (Anderson et al. 2007) was measured with the knee bent 50°.

- Cresswell AG, Löscher WN, Thorstensson A (1995). Influence of gastrocnemius muscle length on triceps surae torque development and electromyographic activity in man. *Exp Brain Res* 105(2):283–290. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7498381/ (snippet: plantarflexion torque falls to about 60% as the knee bends; check the angle convention)
- Kovács B, Csala D, Yang S, Tihanyi J, Gu Y, Hortobágyi T (2024). Knee position affects medial gastrocnemius and soleus activation during dynamic plantarflexion. *Biology Open*. https://doi.org/10.1242/bio.061810 (snippet: about 31% lower torque with the knee bent 90°)
- Influence of knee positions on torque and myoelectric activity of triceps surae at maximum isometric plantar flexion. *Rev Bras Educ Fís Esporte*. https://revistas.usp.br/rbefe/en/article/view/84062 (authors not captured; snippet: 100 vs 73 Nm, knee straight vs bent 90°)

## Rectus femoris: knee extension, hip angle

Probably no correction needed (the snippets report little or no difference), but worth confirming for the leg extension and the squat.

- Bampouras TM, Reeves ND, Baltzopoulos V, Maganaris CN (2017). The role of agonist and antagonist muscles in explaining isometric knee extension torque variation with hip joint angle. *Eur J Appl Physiol* 117(10):2039–2045. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-017-3693-y (snippet: seated 241 vs supine 245 Nm)
- Black et al. (1993). Influence of hip position on electromyographic and torque productions in the knee flexors and extensors. https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU199501064815 (snippet: knee-flexion torque higher sitting, knee extension unchanged)
- Worrell TW, Perrin DH, Denegar CR (1989). The influence of hip position on quadriceps and hamstring peak torque and reciprocal muscle group ratio values. *J Orthop Sports Phys Ther* 11(3):104–107. (no link found; search jospt.org)

## Elbow flexors: grip

Already used: Guenzkofer et al. 2012, Kohn et al. 2018, Kleiber et al. 2015. These would help size the reverse-curl brachioradialis modifier and settle neutral vs supinated strength.

- Boland MR, Spigelman T, Uhl TL (2008). The function of brachioradialis. *J Hand Surg Am* 33(10):1853–1859. https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/the-function-of-brachioradialis/
- Mandalidis DG, O'Brien M. Isokinetic strength of the elbow flexors with the forearm in supination and in the neutral position. *Isokinetics and Exercise Science*. https://content.iospress.com/articles/isokinetics-and-exercise-science/ies00074 (snippet: supinated stronger than neutral, unlike Kohn et al.)
- Marcolin G et al. (2018). Differences in electromyographic activity of biceps brachii and brachioradialis while performing three variants of curl. *PeerJ* 6:e5165. https://peerj.com/articles/5165 (check which variant is the hammer curl)
- Naito A et al. (1995). Electromyographic (EMG) study of the elbow flexors during supination and pronation of the forearm. *Tohoku J Exp Med* 175(4):285–288. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7570586/ (one subject, qualitative)
