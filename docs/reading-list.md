### Brachioradialis share with palms down (reverse-curl modifier, now 1.2× estimated)

- **Coratella G, Tornatore G, Longo S, Toninelli N, Padovan R, Esposito F, Cè E (2023).** Biceps brachii and brachioradialis excitation in biceps curl exercise: different handgrips, different synergy. *Sports* 11(3):64. https://doi.org/10.3390/sports11030064 (open access).
  - Snippet: in 8RM curls, brachioradialis excitation was slightly *higher* palms up than palms down (+5%) or neutral (+6%).
  - If confirmed, the reverse-curl modifier should go to about 1.0 or below. This paper matters most of the three in this section.
- **Chen Y, Huang S, Shen P, et al. (2025).** Forearm rotation and elbow angle differentially modulate biceps brachii and brachioradialis muscle stiffness and EMG activity during low-load isometric contractions. *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil*. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-025-01226-y
- *The role of the muscle brachioradialis in elbow flexion: an EMG study* (ResearchGate 323203359; authors not captured).

### Horizontal abduction (rear-delt) strength in adults (now Mayer's abduction peak as a stand-in)

- **Lategan L (2002).** *Normative isokinetic torque values for rehabilitation in South Africa.* DPhil thesis, University of Pretoria. https://repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/27346
  - 444 men on a Cybex 340, including horizontal abduction and adduction (Nm/kg). This one would close the gap.
- **"Isokinetic dynamometry in measuring bilateral shoulder peak torque: comparison and prediction"**, An-Najah University journal (authors not captured). 23 men, horizontal abduction and adduction at 60°/s.
- Already read: Silva et al. 2006 (*Br J Sports Med* 40:513–517) give a horizontal abduction/adduction ratio of about 0.77, but in junior tennis players (12–18 years). So it is a cross-check only.
