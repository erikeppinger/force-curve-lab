Finger by finger

FingerShareKnuckle / PIP / tipFDP / FDSA2 / A4 of limitEffort

Fingertips line up on the edge (pads a few mm deeper or shallower); each finger keeps close to the grip's PIP and fingertip angles and takes up its length at the knuckle, the ring and little finger also by cupping the palm, and the hand tilts sideways (wrist or arm) and rolls (forearm rotation) if that helps. The block is shared for least effort. A finger that can't reach without a contorted posture stays off the edge. Physics only: see how the model works.

Your hand: finger bone lengths

Typical lengths (mean of 10 hands, Lin et al. 1989) in mm, joint centre to joint centre. Enter your own to see how your fingers meet the edge and which edge profile fits you. A rough way to measure: bend the joints to 90° and measure on the back of the hand from knuckle to knuckle (this slightly overestimates). "Knuckle back" is how far each knuckle sits back towards the wrist from the middle finger's, with the fingers straight in line with the hand (a ruler across the knuckles shows it; the typical values are estimates).

Resistance vs strength

    Force on the hand over one lift

    Play runs one lift: the pull builds while the block still rests on the floor, the block comes off the floor (force above its weight while it speeds up), is held (exactly its weight), set down (the force dips, then peaks as it is slowed) and released. The fingers, wrist, elbow and shoulder all carry this same pull. Dashed: the block's weight.

    Effort across the range

    Joint torque ÷ strength at each angle. The peak is the sticking point.
